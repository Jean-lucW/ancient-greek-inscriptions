## English translation

**Part 1**

Text A (OGIS 506)

Emperor Caesar, son of the God Hadrian,

grandson of the God Trajan Parthicus, great-grandson

of the god Nerva, Titus Aelius Hadrian

Antoninus Augustus, pontifex

maximus, tribunician power 20 times (157 AD) ,

acclaimed emperor twice, [consul four times, father of the]

[fatherland], to the Panhellenion, greetings:

[that the] Panhellenes before you

declared Eurykles to be

fair, I learnt from the letters

sent by them. May you be well. (This letter was sent) one day before

the kalends of December (30 November) , from Rome.

**Part 2**

Text B (OGIS 507)

The archon of the Panhellenes and priest of the God Hadrian Panhellenios

and competition-director of the Great Panhellenia, Claudius Iason (157/8-161/2) , and the

Panhellenes to the Greeks in Asia, greetings:

We have already expressed the worth of Marcus Ulpius Apuleius Eurykles of Aizanoi

through other letters of testimony from us, having sent letters about him

to you yourselves and to his fatherland and to the greatest

emperor, but we considered it just, as the mighty Claudius Iason assumed

the archonship (of the Panhellenes), to give the same testimony namely that he (i.e. Eurykles) has employed fairness and all reverence

about the citizenship of his fellow Panhellenes and that he advanced even further his pre-existing distinction,

which goes back in his family to the beginning, in what

he has continuously said and done through the whole period of the session.

I pray that you fare well.

**Part 3**

Text C (OGIS 505)

The Council of the Areopagos and its herald and competition-director

of the games of the Augusta, Nummius Menis,

to the Officials, Council and People of the Aizanitans, greetings:

Your citizen, Marcus Ulpius Eurykles, the most noteworthy,

was resident in our city for all this time in a manner worthy of

his own distinction and of your city, having spent his time

in Athens, as if it were his fatherland, conversing in an educated manner

and showing a thoroughly excellent attitude

through his zeal for what is most beautiful and revered. And for this we honoured

him with fitting honours and the dedication of a statue and a portrait

both in our fatherland, Athens, in whatever place he wishes, and

among you. And we considered it just to give this same testimony

of the man before you, on account of his decorum and his character and his

love of honour regarding education

**Part 4**

Text D (OGIS 504)

The archon of the Panhellenes and priest of the God Hadrian Panhellenios

and competition-director of the Great Panhellenia, Titus [Flavius Kyllos] (152/3-156/7)

and the Panhellenes to the Council and the People of the Aizanitans, greetings:

And considering it fitting for themselves to display the honours for good men

enduringly and to rejoice with you at the possession of such citizens,

understanding the following, the sort of person this Ulpius Eurykles is, who

has behaved as a fellow citizen to us for the whole period of the session moderately, and as to how

he has won each of us over through friendship and has made himself stand out in the collective

for his education and other excellence and fairness, we considered it

reasonable to give testimony about him to you and to please you by demonstrating

the good will we bear towards him, since both regarding the League of the Panhellenion and privately

regarding our most marvellous archon Flavius Kyllos he has employed a love of honour which

adorns not just Eurykles himself, but also your eminent

city, and the whole time he has continuously spoken and acted in ways

that are worthy of it (i.e. the city) and of his family and of the manly virtue which he gets from his ancestors. And we also

sent letters about him both to the people and to the most divine emperor, having considered him

worthy of even that kind of testimonial. Farewell.
