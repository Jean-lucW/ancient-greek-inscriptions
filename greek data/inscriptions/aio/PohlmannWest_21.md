## English translation

**Part 1**

Paian and [prosodion] for the god, which Limenios son of Thoinos [of Athens] composed [and accompanied with the cithara]

**Column 1**

Come, to this far-seen, Parnassian,

[choir-loving], double-peaked slope, and conduct my hymns,

Pierides, who inhabit Helikon’s snowclad rocks.

Celebrate gold-haired Pythios with songs, the far-shooting and lyre-playing

Phoibos, whom blessed Leto bore by the famous lake,

clinging with her hands [in her struggle] to the blossoming [branch] of a grey

olive tree.

The whole celestial sphere rejoiced, [cloudless and bright],

and the aether made calm the winds’ fast-flying

tracks. Nereus’ [mighty] deep-roaring surf subsided

and with him the great Okeanos who encloses the Earth on all sides

with wet embraces.

Then, leaving the island of Kynthia, [the god] set foot in Attica,

famous for its first fruits, on the earthen [peak] of Tritonis.

The Libyan [flute sounded clearly] pouring out its honey-breathing tone,

mixing its sweet voice with varying [melodies of the cithara],

while the rock-dwelling Echo cried out [‘Paian, ie Paian!’]. He was

full of joy at accepting and [recognizing] Zeus’ immortal [will]. Therefore,

from this beginning, we invoke Paian, the [whole]

earthborn people, as well as Bacchos’ great, sacred, thyrsus-striking [swarm] of artists,

dwelling in the Kekropian city. But you

who hold the fortune-singing tripod, step on [this] Parnassian ridge, that is trodden by the gods

and loves divine inspiration. And, having braided a sprig of

laurel around your wine-dark curls,

dragging [immense foundations] with your immortal hand, [you], Lord,

[challenge Ge’s monstrous] daughter. But you, Leto’s [offspring with passionate]

[eyes], killed both Gaia’s [savage] child, with arrows, [and likewise Tityos],

**Column 2**

because he had a desire for your mother, [...]

beast, to which you killed [...]

a pipe-song from [friendly [...] ]

[when] you protected [the sanctuary] of Gaia, [at the navel (of the world), o Lord],

when the barbarian Ares, [showing no reverence] and trying to plunder

your prophetic, [well-hidden seat], perished in a dank [snowstorm]. [But, o Phoibos,]

preserve the god-founded [city] of Pallas [and its renowned people, and along with him],

you, Goddess, mistress of bows and Cretan [dogs, Artemis], and you,

most honourable [Leto], and [take care of] the inhabitants of Delphi together with [the children]

living with them, that they shall not stumble in their halls, and

come kindly to the aid of Bacchos’ servants, [winners of sacred contests],

and expand the empire of the Romans, spear-crowned by unaging [power],

flourishing and victorious.
