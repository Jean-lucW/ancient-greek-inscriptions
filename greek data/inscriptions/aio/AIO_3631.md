**Part 2**

Second stele

**Fragment e**

(1) [Appeals, which] Ael(ius) Praxagoras, Cl(audius) Demostratos, and Ael(ius) Themison made against Ael(ius) Dionysios [are shown] to be justified,

[so it was] necessary to hold these appeal trials. It was decided that it was enough to [know] the elections were held in accordance with the law,

and I did not consider it to be necessary that [judgement?] of these men take place, due to it not having happened in Athens either. [For]

next time, however, in order to leave no doubt, those voluntarily seeking the post of Dadouch or any other

priesthood greater than what they have got already, must first surrender the headband (of that priesthood) in accordance with the law, but if anyone

should be called up [by] the People, there will be no grounds for dispute against him, if the previous symbols are not surrendered before

it is accepted. Once elected, however, he too will surrender the priesthood previously belonging to him. (2) Appeals, which

Sentius Attalos and Klemes son of Klemes and Claudius Chrysippos made from the court and from the king Cl(audius) Eupraxides

against Valerius Mamertinus will be closed. So, since Mamertinus, although he is a Eumolpid, had no

parent on either side from the genos of the Kerykes, he lacks the only means by which those from one of these

[two] gene are allowed to transfer to the other. He will desist from seeking the position of sacred herald. But the elections

between the others – both those who have already received judgement in their favour and those who now intend to announce their candidacies in accordance with the laws of the Athenians – will be resumed. Mamertinus will not be removed from the number of the Eumolpids and

will recover his priesthood. But this idea, which came to me after I had reached my verdict on the present case, should not [in]

future confuse the careful observance of the rules. (3) Ladikos son of Polyainos the appellant against Sophanes son of

Sophanes from Julius Damostratos the archon of the Panhellenion has clearly been called for judgement already in the set time

within which it is possible to challenge those elected as Panhellenes and, having been denied,

since, although the election happened after the traditionally fixed time, he had not yet

reached the legal age and had not held any magistracy at any point previously, as the God my grandfather (i.e. Emperor Hadrian) ordained, he seems

to have appealed unjustifiably. (4) Epigonos son of Epiktetos, having appealed from Julius Damostratos against Eudemos son of Aphrodeisios,

will have his trial before my Quintilii regarding his membership in the Panhellenion. Indeed, he himself asked to be sent

to get judgement from them, and it is clear that many of the aspects pertaining to me in the affairs of Greece

will be completed by them. (5) Athenodoros son of Agrippas, having appealed from Papius Rufus, who was archon

of the Panhellenes in the previous five-year-period, against the administrators of the mighty Cl(audius) Herodes, since he

provided neither the minutes of the Panhellenes nor the verdict that was delivered, will have his trial before my Quintilii,

so that he not be compelled after so long to wait for the moments in which it is possible for me in the midst of my military activities

to decide matters requiring judgement. (6) Nostimos son of Dionysios did not show that he has become an Areopagite

in accordance with the laws, but if there is in this matter a way that he is able to be enrolled in the Areopagos Council, the Quintilii

will decide. The grounds on which he could be expelled from the membership of the council of the Panhellenes, [seem to have been]

demonstrated by me, when I decided the appeal against Euphras son of Nikon. (7) Popilius [Pius]

remains in legitimate possession of Athenian citizenship, as the Areopagites recognised, [for]

confirmation of the status is necessary for him and for the others who, seeking the permission granted in the event of destruction [of a]

certified copy, presented their just claims to the Areopagites. But in the future, [in accordance with]

the laws and in accordance with the ancestral customs it will be carefully monitored and scrutinised whether someone is an [Athenian]

by descent. (8) Appeal which Ael(ius) Praxagoras made from Gavinius Saturninus, in connection with the trial, in which, once the

‘codicils’ in the name of Stratolaos had been brought forward, the investigation took place, is shown to be

legitimate. If, however, within two months after the reading of this letter in Athens, anyone should wish either publicly or privately

to enter into a case about the gift, which they purported to be owed to the city,

this shall be permitted to him, with the scheduled time for judgement by my Quintilii being set

within two more months, but if this does not occur within this interval, no one may attempt

to take up the controversy at a later time. Praxagoras will return to possession of the estates and

will get arbitration about their profits from the mighty Quintilii. As for the estates, which were said

to have been left to the mighty Herodes, he will now return to them. About the profits, Ingenuus will judge. But if Praxagoras

complains about the slowness of the transfer, the matter will be brought forward by the leaders of the people. In response to the petition of the

Athenians, which they presented on account of the father’s age and him being the only man left to the house, I would be able

to allow that Pheidimos be summoned back from the island to which he was exiled for scrutiny of the matter,

but that he stay outside the borders of Attika in the future. (9) Agathokles son of Agathokles’ claim for the embassy, [which]

he is carrying out before me: it will indeed be repaid by his fatherland and the board of the Areopagites, along with the deposits of the appeal cases,

which the administrators of the estates of the mighty Herodes brought against Ael(ius) Ameinias; likewise also the deposits of the appeals, which

Ael(ius) Praxagoras, Cl(audius) Demostratos, Ael(ius) Themison, Sentius

Attalos, Klemes son of Klemes, Valerius Mamertinus, Ladikos son of Polyainos, Euodos son of Onesimos, Nostimos son of

[Dio]n[y]sios, Popilius Peius made for the cases they argued before me, will be repaid. I said previously that Epigonos and Athenodoros’ appeals had

to be closed. If any other appeal trials dependent on this judgement, about which I did not rule,

have occurred before publication (i.e. of these decisions), they will close them, to be scrutinised by the judge on the same terms

as they were going to be scrutinised on and with no right to an appeal case; Ingenuus

will write to me regarding whose cases these may be. If any cases were delivered after the decision, he will hear them in the

appropriate order. (10) How much concern I feel for the reputation of Athens, that it possess its ancient

awesomeness, I think I have made clear enough also when I attempted to restore its

selective board to its ancient custom, according to which they accepted into the Areopagos only those

who met the standard of the three-generation rule; and if only there were a surfeit of glorious clans, so that it were possible for me even now

to continue to maintain our position, but since they, taking into account the events that have happened by chance,

in response to which I know many other cities have petitioned urgently for a remedy, must on the one hand return

to benevolence regarding the past and on the other hand make things viable for the future, having agreed with the Athenians to this extent, I will concede to them, regarding the past, to focus only on whether

someone’s father is well-born, and even if it should appear that one of the members of the Areopagos ought to be expelled under the three-generation rule, he will recover the rank,

and after this it will be enough that someone be well-born from . For those who are sons of a freedman

father and have broken into the Areopagos after our letter,

it is time to desist from their folly. If, however, anyone born of a freedman father was not expelled before my letter by any court

capable of returning a verdict on these matters, he will stay. Any who, after our letter, on account of their

grandfather having been a freedman, either were removed from the Areopagos Council or voluntarily renounced the honour, are still

barred. However, those who withdraw from the board for the aforesaid reasons in future should not on account of this

be disturbed from participating in other honours and ranks. If there should be anyone enrolled contrary to our

commands, who cannot sit in the Areopagos after the decision that has now been delivered,

they shall depart from it within a year, and whoever continues to remain even after this has been announced [or even]

worms in contrary to what has been written above, will be ranked equal to those expelled by the court, [both] in lesser matters

from this and in pursuit of the lesser ranks and honours. If any of the Areopagites currently among the Panhellenes

should be found unable to fulfil the three-generation rule, they will not be expelled

from the board due to this, but in future no one else from the Areopagites will be enrolled among the Panhellenes

except those who are able to enter the elections because they fulfil the three-generation rule. Regarding the Council

of the Five Hundred, it is enough that it be arranged so that those who are enrolled are themselves well-born.

(11) Ingenuus himself will be the judge for the disputes from both of the parties and he will also judge

for those taking care of the estates of the mighty man (i.e. Herodes) against any who make no counter-claim themselves.

Those who have administered the public money of the city and owe accounts have my Quintilii

as judges, before whom the Athenians’ legal representatives will take care to present their own sums in full.

Ingenuus will judge those Athenians needing a judgement at Eleusis and Piraeus, at whatever time he wishes

at each, while for those from Sparta he will fix a place in the nearby cities, in accordance with what

he himself determines to be most convenient. (12) I think that it is clear from what I have declared that I have provided for each matter not

from authority more than from concern, in order that in future it may be possible for Herodes to

participate with shared happiness in divine and human affairs among the Athenians, with his famous zeal for education, and that the Athenians,

remembering the once-famed generosity of the mighty Herodes towards themselves, may renew the warmth of their former

relationship with each other, for which they do not need me as mediator. For what could still linger in anyone’s opinion

after erasing the memory of the causes for complaint, given the service that has been rendered on all points, [so that]

the Athenians could love Herodes, who is mine and theirs, since no other great matter still stands in the way of good will? (13) However, once all these rulings about the judgements had been composed in the Greek language,

As for an additional matter I noticed that was omitted in what I had declared, capable of being inferred from my verdict, even if not [from]

the words of the declaration, but requiring some exposition so that no one would

create a [new] dispute, I thought it was fitting to delineate this clearly: if someone born of freedman

left the Council of the Five Hundred, it shall be allowed for them to return for scrutiny, on the same terms as for those from the Areopagos, and those from the

Council of the Five Hundred shall demonstrate that they themselves were born free, for it is not necessary that those born to freedman fathers

be barred, but only those who are themselves freedmen, which matter is determined for the future regarding them.
