# Council decree on repairs to the Asklepieion, 51/0 BC

## English translation

**Part 1**

For the good fortune of the Council and People of the Athenians. In the

archonship of Lysandros son of Apolexis (52/1) , in the twelfth prytany,

of Pandionis , for which Gaios son of Gaios of Halai was secretary.

Twenty-third day of Skirophorion, twenty-third day

of the prytany. Council in the Council chamber. Of the presiding committee,

Theangelos son of Theangelos of Aithalidai was putting to the vote, and

his fellow members of the presiding committee. The Council decided. Meniskos son of Philokles

of Kolonai proposed: since, having made an approach to the

Council, the allotted priest of Asklepios and Hygieia

for the year after the archonship of Lysandros (i.e. 51/0) , Diokles son of Diokles of Kephisia the

younger demonstrates that the doorways of what was

formerly the entrance to the sanctuary, are in ruins, similarly also the

roof behind the gateway and even the temple of the ancient

foundation of Asklepios and of Hygieia (are in ruins), and, owing to this,

he requests the Council to permit him, in repairing (the sanctuary) from his own

resources, to install doors on the old gateway, and to roof the

back part of the gateway, and (repair) the temple opposite

the entrance, in order to restore the sanctuary to its old state. For good

fortune, the Council shall decide to permit Diokles son of Diokles of Kephisia

the younger to make the dedication of the doors

and to roof the back part of the gateway, and to repair

the old <temple>, just as he requests, and to make this

inscription on the doors and the roof: “Diokles

son of Diokles of Kephisia, the younger, having become priest in the

year of the archonship of Lysiades (51/0) , dedicated the doors and the

roof at the back of the gateway to Asklepios and Hygieia and the People,”

and on the temple, similarly: “Diokles son of Diokles of

Kephisia the younger, having become priest in the year of the archonship of

Lysiades (51/0) , having repaired the temple from his own resources,

dedicated (this) to Asklepios and Hygieia and the <People>,” so that,

these things being accomplished, the Council may behave piously in matters relating to the divine,

and others may become zealots for the same things.
