# Honours for priests and religious officials

## English translation

**Part 1**

[...]

[...] proposed: concerning what the priest of Dionysos

[in the Piraeus, Meixigenes?] and the religious officials appointed

by the Council [reported] about the sacrifices that they made to Dionysos and

to the other gods to whom it was fitting to sacrifice on behalf of the Athenian Council and

People, for good fortune, the Council shall resolve:

that the presiding committee allotted to preside in the People

at the next Assembly shall introduce the priest and

the religious officials to the People, and put their report on the

agenda, and submit the opinion of the Council to the People,

that it seems good to the Council, that the People should accept the good things,

which the priest and the religious officials report as having occurred

in the sacrifices which they made to Dionysos and to the other gods,

for the health and preservation of the Athenian Council and People

and of the children and women and other possessions

of the Athenians; and to praise the priest of Dionysos,

Meixigenes of Cholleidai, and of Poseidon Pelagios,

Himeraios of Phaleron, and of Zeus Soter, Nikokles

of Hagnous, and of Ammon, Pausiades of Phaleron,

for their love of honour towards the Council and their piety

towards the gods, and crown each of them with a gold

crown of 500 drachmas when they have rendered their accounts;

and since the religious officials chosen by the Council

managed well and with love of honour the supply of requirements for the rites

and [...] of the sacrifices [...] of the heroes and

in other respects concerning the [...]

have managed [...] justly and with love of honour, to praise the religious officials,

Eunomos of Euonymon , Sybarites of Gargettos , Gnosias

of Kydathenaion , Phileas of Paionidai , Chairephanes of Sphettos ,

Apollodoros of Ptelea , Autosthenes of Xypete ,

Amiantos of Auridai , Epikrates of Aphidna , Philostratos of Pallene ,

for their excellence and justice towards the Athenian Council and

People and their management concerning the rites,

and to crown each of them with a gold crown of 500

drachmas, when they have rendered their accounts; and the treasurer

of the People shall also give them for a sacrifice and dedication [] drachmas

from the People’s fund for expenditure on decrees; and the

secretary of the Council shall inscribe this decree on a stone

stele and stand it in the theatre of Dionysos ( erased text: [in

Piraeus?]); and for inscribing the stele the treasurer

of the People shall give [] drachmas from the People’s

fund for expenditure on decrees.
