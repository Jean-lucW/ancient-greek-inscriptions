# Decree of Rhamnous and Athenian residents honouring the general Archandros of Eleusis, 248/7 BC

## English translation

**Part 1**

[...] having been elected by the People

[general and] [...] assigned to the coastal

territory, having performed well the management

[...] and [...]

[...] of the citizens in the fort; and again in

the archonship of Diomedon (248/7) , when the People elected him general,

in time of war, on coming to the fort, having first

carried out an inspection, he assigned the older men

among both the citizens and the metics to guard the fort

together with the guards, and placed the younger men

among the citizens on watch, and despatched the paroikoi and soldiers

to occupy positions in the countryside for a distance

up to sixty stades and accomplished the harvesting

of the arboreal crops; and he established

effective hidden agents in the most advantageous lookouts

from the number of soldiers under his command,

so that the grazing animals and the bodies (i.e. slaves) might be kept safe; and when

there was a shortage of grain in the fort, he took care that, of the

wheat being given out in the city, some of it was conveyed to the fort

safely, and some of it was sold there and bought

cheaply by both the guards and those citizens residing

in Rhamnous; and he made advance payments of money for

the rations from his own resources, so that each man might be well-disciplined

in his performance of the duties assigned to him, and by these means he protected

the territory around Rhamnous throughout the year of his generalship, although the rest of the countryside

was infested by pirates; and he continues to say and do

what is in the interest of the , being

useful to the citizens both collectively and individually; for good fortune, the Rhamnousians

shall decide, and the other citizens stationed in the

fort, to praise Archandros son of Kallippos of Eleusis

and crown him with a gold crown according to the law for the excellence

and justice he continues to show the Rhamnousians

and those living at Rhamnous, so that there shall be

an incentive, for all those appointed generals with responsibility

for the fort and the countryside to behave with love of honour

in the common interest; and to inscribe this decree

on a stone stele and stand it by the gate; and for inscribing the

stele to elect three men who will carry out

what has been decreed. The following were chosen: Lysikleides

son of Nikostratos of Rhamnous [...]
