**Part 1**

Gods.

Priest of Ares and Athena

Areia, Dion son of Dion of Acharnai dedicated (this).

Oath of the ephebes

Ancestral oath of the ephebes, which the ephebes must swear: 'I shall not disgrace the sacred weapons nor shall I abandon the man beside me wherever I am stationed; and I shall fight in defence of both sacred and holy things, and I shall not hand over the fatherland lessened, but greater and better, both by my own efforts and together with all, and I shall be obedient to those who are at any time in authority, prudently, and to the laws that are

established and those which they may establish in the future, prudently; and if anyone destroys (them), I shall not permit it, both by my own efforts and together with all, and I shall honour the ancestral sacred rites. Witnesses ⟦o⟧

the gods Aglauros, Hestia, Enyo, Enyalios, Ares and Athena Areia, Zeus, Thallo, Auxo, Hegemone, Herakles, the boundaries of the fatherland, wheat,

barley, vines, olives, figs.'

Oath of Plataia

Oath which the Athenians swore when they were about

to fight against the barbarians:

'I shall fight while I live, and I shall not consider it more important

to live than to be free, and I shall not desert the taxiarch nor the enomotarch, whether living or dead, and I shall not depart unless the leaders

lead (us) away, and I shall do whatever the generals command, and the dead of those who fought as allies I shall bury in the same place and shall leave no one unburied; and having conquered the barbarians in battle I shall tithe the city of the Thebans, and I shall not destroy Athens nor Sparta nor Plataia nor any of the other cities which fought as allies, nor shall I overlook them being constrained by hunger, nor shall I cut them off from running water, whether they are friends or enemies. And if I abide by what is written in the oath, may my city be free from disease, but if not, may it be diseased; and may my city be unsacked, but if not, may it be sacked; and may my (land) bear (fruit), but if not, may it be barren; and may women bear children resembling their parents, but if not, monsters; and may flocks bear young resembling flocks, but if not, monsters.' Having sworn these things, covering the sacrificial victims with their shields, at the sound of the trumpet they made a curse, that if they transgressed any of the

things sworn and did not abide by what is written in the oath, there should be a curse on those who had sworn it.
