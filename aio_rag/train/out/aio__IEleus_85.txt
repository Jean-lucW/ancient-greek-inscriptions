Epigenes proposed: for the good fortune of the demesmen;

since Philokomos made a proposal to the demesmen

about Akris, to lease out for the god the

quarrying, so that the sacrifice may be as fine

as possible, and Moirokles has bought (the lease) from the demesmen

for five years for three half-minas each

year and has contributed in addition a hundred drachmas for the five

years; the Eleusinians shall decide: to praise

Philokomos son of Phalanthides and crown him with a gold

crown for his excellence and good will towards

the demesmen, and for the crown Moirokles shall give the money

to Philokomos in the presence of the demesmen,

a hundred drachmas; and to praise

Moirokles son of Euthydemos, because he takes care for the demesmen

that the revenue may be as great as possible,

and crown him with a foliage crown. uninscribed space

Philokomos son of Phalanthides of Eleusis proposed: for the good fortune

of the demesmen; so that for Herakles in Akris the revenue

may be as great as possible and the sacrifice may be made as fine as possible, the demesmen

shall resolve: the quarries at Eleusis, which

[...] are sacred to Herakles in Akris,

the demarch shall lease in the assembly of the demesmen to the one

offering the most; and the lessee shall pay

the rent, in the archonship of Niketes (332/1) at whatever

time he persuades the demesmen, before the sacrifice, and after the archonship of

Niketes in the month of Metageitnion at the

elections, when the demesmen hold their assembly in the Theseion;

and the lessee shall provide two men as guarantors,

[who shall swear] that he will truly pay the whole rent in the

time stated; and the demarch, having received this

money, shall provide it for the festival of Herakles

in Akris; and the demesmen shall vote by show of hands immediately

whether it seems good to lease for a year or for a longer

time, and whichever seems good, this shall be valid, and the demarch

shall lease on these terms; and it shall not be possible for anyone

to propose that this money should be diverted anywhere else than to

the sacrifice of Herakles, nor for the hieromnemones to put it to the vote,

nor for the demarch; and if anyone either proposes or puts to the vote anything

contrary to this decree, he shall owe to the god double

whatever he proposes or puts to the vote; and the scrutineer and the

advocate shall compulsorily condemn them to pay this

money or owe it themselves; and the demarch shall inscribe this

decree on a stone stele and stand it

in the sanctuary of Herakles in Akris, so that what has been

decreed by the demesmen may be valid for all time

and not be annulled; and the priest of Herakles, Antiphanes, shall join in taking care of the stele, so that

it is set up in the sanctuary

before the sacrifice in the archonship of Niketes; and for

the inscribing of the stele the demarch shall give ten

drachmas from the revenue of the god. And this decree shall be valid

from the archonship of Niketes, from whatever day the

demesmen vote it. uninscribed space
