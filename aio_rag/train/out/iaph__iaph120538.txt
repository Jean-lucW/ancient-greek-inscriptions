For good fortune.

Marcus Ulpius Appuleius Eurykles, high-priest of Asia designated

of the temples in Smyrna for the second time, to the magistrates, Council and People of the Aphrodisians, greetings.

Since you wished me to take forethought also for matters concerning the contests,

both on account of piety towards the greatest Emperor Marcus Aurelius

Commodus Antoninus Augustus and on account of the memory of those who made the bequests

and on account of the glory of the city, and since members of the guild have already

often petitioned me, I did not leave this part unexamined either,

following the order and zeal of my curatorship, since until now the establishment

of the contests has been lacking on account of the provision

of the funds, which ought to be increased together both according to the intentions of the deceased

and according to the standing of the endowment from which the contests must

be held.

Now the contest from the will of Flavius Lysimachos has reached

a capital endowment of twelve myriads (of denarii), so as to be able from these

every four years to fund the musical contest, as the

testator decided; and the sums beyond the twelve myriads, which are out

on loan, and the interest that has accrued to them up to the beginning of the year,

make a capital of three myriads of denarii and one thousand eight hundred

and thirty-nine denarii. You are able, therefore, at the beginning of the year to hold this

contest, for good fortune, with prizes in the talent category

and competitions according to the prizes; and the fixed term for the

future and the coming four-year period will be the period

from the [Bar]billeia [celebrated] at Ephesus to the [Koina?] of Asia. [Farewell?]
