[...] [to Deme?]ter

[...] elder

[...] as follows:

[since] Alex[imachos son of Sosist]ratos, one of our

citizens, continually

makes the greatest provision on behalf of

our city, and has become the cause of many and great

benefits both in common

and to each individually, making his conduct

towards the crowds and cities

reasonable and humane,

and shows by his actions the nobility of his own family

from the beginning, on every

occasion acting worthily of the policy of his ancestors,

and making the finest provision in word and deed,

and devoting himself

to the interests of the city, thus

having made a beginning among us, he

contributed money during the crisis of the

war for the guarding

of the city, being second to none of the citizens

in the restoration of the fatherland,

as the honours given to him in the most conspicuous

places already make clear; and during the

visits of the generals

leaving nothing undone as far as

is in his power, helping

both his own fatherland and the

citizens, he displays his

good will towards all; and at the time of the war

against the Libyans, when grain was scarce

and the city was eager

to send grain-buyers

to Leptis, seeing in these circumstances too

the distress of his own fellow-citizens

because of the crisis of the time, wishing

no less to take part

in affairs, being an exceedingly patriotic

and good-loving man

of reputation and excellence, seeing that the difference

was small, but acting in accordance with the virtues

written down in the laws, seeing

the price of grain rising,

he funded the grain-purchase so that it was sold

in portions at talents: tal., tal., 30; and being eager

to bring in the grain quickly,

he magnanimously undertook, in preference to the increase

of his own money,

to perform a public service for the safety of the crowds;

and it is just and right

that good men

receive honour from that and meet with

greater acceptance, so that the others also,

seeing that the city's gratitude

is unfailing,

devoting themselves to the interests

of the city, may obtain the same

benefits; the gerontes

and the Council shall decide to praise Ale-

ximachos son of Sosistratos for the

benefactions done by

him, and in addition to the existing

honours the city shall set up for him a bronze

statue in the agora of the gods

in whatever place he himself chooses, having

this inscription: "Aleximachos

son of Sosistratos of Arsinoe, for his excellence

and good will which he continually has towards

the city, the Arsinoeans (dedicated this)"; and the ephors

shall set up the statue

and a stele of white marble beside it

bearing a copy of this

decree; and the expense

incurred for the stele the treasurers shall give from

public funds, 600 dr.

White (votes): 109.
