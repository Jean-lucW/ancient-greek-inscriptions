For the good fortune of the Council and the People of the Athenians. In the archonship of Ly-

sandros son of Apolexis (52/1), in the twelfth prytany, of Pandionis,

for which Gaius son of Gaius of Halai was secre-

tary. On the twenty-third of Skirophorion, the twenty-third

of the prytany. Council in the Council chamber. Of the presiding committee

Theangelos son of Theangelos of Aithalidai was putting to the vote and his fellow

presiding committee members. The Council decided. Meniskos son of Philokles of Kolo-

nos proposed: since, having made an approach to the

Council, the allotted priest of Asklepios and Hygieia for the

year after the archonship of Lysandros, Diokles son of Diokles of Kephi-

sia the younger, reports that the doors have been ruined of the

former entrance to the sanctuary, and likewise also the roof behind

the gateway, and furthermore also the temple of the ancient

foundation of Asklepios and Hygieia, and for this reason he

calls on the Council to allow him, providing them from his

own resources, to fit doors to the ancient gateway, and to roof also the

rear part of the gateway and the temple opposite the

entrance, so that the ancient order may be restored to the sanctuary; for good

fortune, the Council shall decide to allow Diokles son of Diokles of Kephi-

sia the younger to make the dedication of the doors

and to roof the rear part of the gateway, and to fit out

also the ancient <temple> as he requests, and to make the in-

scription on the doors and the roof as follows: "Diokles

son of Diokles of Kephisia the younger, having become priest in the year

of the archonship of Lysiades (51/0), dedicated the doors and the roof behind

the gateway to Asklepios and Hygieia and the People";

and on the temple likewise: "Diokles son of Diokles of Ke-

phisia the younger, having become priest in the year of the archonship of Lysiades,

having fitted out the temple from his own

resources, dedicated it to Asklepios and Hygieia and the <People>"; so that, these things being

accomplished, the Council may be seen to conduct its relations with the divine piously,

and others also may become emulators of similar conduct.
