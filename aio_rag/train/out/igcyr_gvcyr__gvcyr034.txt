The leader of the youths, the young son of Dionysios,

this marble stone proclaims to men;

and with all its swift hooves the horse gave him,

having been crowned, a complete victory with the chariot;

nor did his fatherland rear him ignorant of the wise words of Hermes

in its gleaming gymnasia,

but well cared for also by Herakles; therefore in the contest

he accomplished deeds equal also to those from the Muses;

for which, Neon son of Karneios, delight of the horse, so much I declare,

the Ouranidai granted you a [statue] [struck?].

The son of Theochrestos, most renowned of young [men],

this inscription proclaims thus also to [mortals] to come,

praised among the Muses, and cared for by [Herakles],

having been crowned with the sprig of a double chariot-driving,

bearing the name of his grandfather Aristarchos; for indeed his begetter

named for his son this ancestral appellation;

O formerly sacred [...] of Greece accomplished;

and he is sent again across the dark [sea];

wherefore long ago great wonder arose at you, [divine Cyrene],

[raising] such sons as athletes.
