[...]

[...] Athenians, [and] took care that as

finely as possible the ambassadors whom the People sent to the King should travel; and to reply to the man who has come from the king of the Sidonians that also

for the future, if he is a good man towards the Athenian People, there is nothing that he will fail to obtain from the Athenians of whatever he may need; and Straton the king of Sidon shall also be proxenos of the Athenian People, both himself

and his descendants; and the secretary of the Council shall inscribe this decree

on a stone stele within ten days and

set it down on the acropolis; and for inscribing the stele the

treasurers shall give to the secretary of the Council 30

drachmas from the ten talents (fund); and the Council shall also make tokens with the king of the Sidonians, so that

the Athenian People may know if anything

the king of the Sidonians sends requesting of the city, and the king of the Sidonians may know when the Athenian People sends anyone to him; and to invite also to hospitality the man who has come from

the king of the Sidonians in the city hall tomorrow.

Menexenos proposed: in other respects in accordance with Kephisodotos; but as many of the Sidonians as, living in Sidon and being citizens there, are staying at Athens for the purpose of trade, it shall not be possible to exact the metic tax from them nor to appoint any of them as choregos

nor to register any

property-based tax against them.
