**Face A**

The Council and the People decided. Kekropis was the prytany; Mnesitheos was secretary; Eupeithes was chairman. Kallias proposed: to repay to the gods

the money that is owed, since the three thousand talents for Athena, which had been voted, have been brought up to the acropolis, in our own coinage; and they shall repay it from the money which has been voted for repayment to the gods, both what is now with the Greek treasurers and the rest which belongs to this

money, and the proceeds of the tithe when it has been sold; and the thirty accountants who are now in office shall reckon precisely what is owed to the gods, and the Council shall have full power to convene the accountants; and

the prytany together with the Council shall repay the money and shall delete (the debts) when they have repaid it, searching out the tablets and the writing tablets and anywhere else they may be written; and the priests and the religious officials and anyone else who knows shall reveal what is written; and treasurers of this money shall be appointed by lot at the same time as the other officials, just like those of the sacred money of Athena; and these shall keep as treasurers on the acropolis in the Opisthodomos the money of the gods, as far as is possible and religiously proper, and they shall join in opening and closing the doors of the Opisthodomos and join in sealing them with the treasurers of Athena; and from the present treasurers and the overseers and the religious officials in the sanctuaries, who now administer it, they shall count out and weigh the money in the presence of the Council on the acropolis, and the treasurers who have been allotted shall receive it from the present

officials, and they shall inscribe it all on a single stele, both for each

of the gods the money, how much belongs to each, and the total of all, separately the silver and the gold; and in future the treasurers in office at any time shall inscribe on a stele and render account, both of the money existing

and of what comes in to the gods and if anything is spent during the year, to the accountants, and they shall submit to audit; and they shall render the account from Panathenaia to Panathenaia, just like those who are treasurers of Athena's property; and the treasurers shall set up on the acropolis the stelai on which they inscribe the sacred money; and when the money has been repaid to the gods,

they shall use the remaining money for the dockyard and the walls [...]
