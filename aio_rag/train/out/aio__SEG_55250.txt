**Part 2**

Letter 1 (Latin)

[In the consulship of M(arcus) Annius Verus for the second time and Cn(aeus) A]rrius Augur.

From Plotina Augusta.

[What my zeal] is for the school of Epicurus, you know very well, lord. Its succession must be assisted by you.

[For now, since] it is not permitted for a successor (diadochos) to be adopted except from among Roman citizens, the [possibility] of choosing is reduced to narrow limits.

[I ask], therefore, in the name of Popillius Theotimos, who is at present successor at Athens, that he be permitted by you both to make his will in Greek

concerning that part of his dispositions which pertains to the ordering of the succession, and to be able to substitute as his successor one of foreign status, if the progress of the person should so recommend; and that what you grant to Theotimos,

the future successors of the school of Epicurus may thereafter enjoy by the same right, all the more because it is the practice,

whenever a mistake has been made by the testator concerning the choice of a successor, that by common counsel the best man is substituted by the adherents of the same school: which will be done more easily if he is chosen from among many.

**Part 3**

Letter 2 (Latin)

Imp(erator) Caesar Trajan Hadrian Aug(ustus) to Popillius Theotimos: I permit you to make your will in Greek concerning those matters which pertain to the succession of the Epicurean school. But since he will also more easily choose a successor if he has the possibility of substituting from among foreigners as well, I grant this also to the others who hold the succession hereafter: it shall be permitted for this right to be transferred either to a foreigner or

to a Roman citizen.

**Part 4**

Letter 3 (Greek)

Plotina Augusta to all the friends, greetings. We have what we were eager to obtain; for it has been granted to the successor, whoever is to lead the succession of Epicurus which is in

Athens, both to arrange by a Greek will all the administration pertaining to the succession and to choose whether he wishes a Greek or a Roman as the one who will preside over the

succession. Since, therefore, a fine power has been added, for which we ought to feel a worthy gratitude to the one who is truly a benefactor and overseer of all education and in this respect a most revered emperor, and to me most dear in all things, both as an outstanding lord and as a good

son, it is fitting that each of those entrusted with the decision on the headship should always try to appoint in his own place the best from among those who share the doctrine and to give more weight

to the view of the whole than to private familiarity with certain persons. It pleased me, then, not to [love some] more than those who seemed to excel in the power of our doctrines and in

the consequent superiority in the disposition of character. But if this should not come about, not because of

the particular nature of the matter, but because of our own weakness or through some other chance

hindrance, I think it is proper that the one who will deliberate on behalf of the common worship should aim at what will please all in common and not himself privately. By Zeus, nor do I think

that one who has taken hold of the benefit that has accrued to him from the doctrines and [is grateful] for such awareness of his, by using reasoning that will not allow [him] to misrepresent the magnitude of the gift, will not be so disposed as also

[to preserve securely] the guarding of the sanctity of that place, which contains the
