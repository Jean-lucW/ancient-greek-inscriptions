**Part 1**

When Eukles was archon at [De]lphi, in the month Poitropios, when the councillors for the

first six-month period were Eudokos son of Praxias and Hagesilaos son of Tarantinos, and the secretary was Politas son of Asandros, and in Athens when the general was

Xenokles, in the month Poseideon, on these terms Xenon son of Philistos

of Athens sold to Apollo Pythios a female slave whose name is Arsinoa,

home-born by birth, for the price of ten silver minas, and he has the whole price,

as Arsinoa entrusted the sale to the god, on condition that she shall be free and not to be claimed by anyone for all her life, doing

whatever she wishes and going away to whomever she wishes. Guarantor

according to the law of the city: Eudokos son of Praxias of Delphi. If anyone should lay hands on

for enslavement

Arsinoa, they shall guarantee to the

god the sale, both the seller Xenon and the guarantor Eudokos; and if they do not

guarantee the sale to the god, they shall be liable to exaction according to the law of the city; and likewise anyone who happens to be present

shall be authorised to rescue Arsinoa as being free, being free from liability and

not subject to any suits and penalties. Witnesses:

Nikolaos son of Timon, Kallimachos and Hybrilaos

sons of Mnasidamos, Andron son of Xenokles, Philistos

son of Xenon, of Chaleion; Dionysios son of Iranion, Babylos son of Aiakidas, Laiadas son of Babylos, Xenon son of Xenokritos, Damophilos son of Herakleidas, Timokritos son of Eukleidas, of Delphi.
