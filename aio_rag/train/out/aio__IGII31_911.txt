The People

(honours) Kallias

son of Thymochares

of Sphettos.

In the archonship of Sosistratos (270/69), in the sixth prytany, of Pandionis,

for which Athenodoros son of Gorgippos of Acharnai was secretary.

On the eighteenth of Posideon, the twenty-first

of the prytany. Principal Assembly. Of the presiding committee Epichares son of Pheidostratos of Erchia

was putting to the vote and his fellow presiding committee members.

The Council and the People decided.

Euchares son of Euarchos of Konthyle proposed: since Kallias,

when the uprising took place by the People against

those occupying the city, and it had expelled the soldiers from the town,

but the fort in the

Mouseion was still occupied and the countryside was at war

with those from Piraeus, and Demetrios was approaching

from the Peloponnese with his army

against the town, Kallias, learning of the danger

to the city and having selected a thousand of the soldiers

stationed with him on Andros, and having

paid them wages and distributed grain rations, came

immediately to the town to help the People, acting in accordance with the

good will of king Ptolemy towards the People, and leading

out into the countryside the soldiers with him he

protected the gathering in of the grain harvest, making every

effort that as much grain as possible should be brought

into the city; and when Demetrios arrived and

encamped around the town and besieged it, Kallias fought

on behalf of the People and made sorties with the soldiers

with him and was wounded, shrinking from no

danger on any occasion for the sake

of the preservation of the People; and when king Ptolemy

sent Sostratos to act in the interests

of the city, and Sostratos summoned an embassy

to him at Piraeus, with which he would conclude the

peace on behalf of the city with Demetrios, Kallias complied

in this with the generals and the Council, and

served as ambassador on behalf of the People and did everything in the interests

of the city, and remained in the town with

the soldiers until the peace was concluded, and having sailed back

to king Ptolemy he co-operated in everything with the embassies

sent by the People

and worked together for the interests of the city;

and when Ptolemy the younger king took over the kingship,

Kallias visited the town, and when the

generals summoned him and disclosed the

circumstances in which the city was and appealed to him to exert

himself on behalf of the city with king Ptolemy so

that some help might come as quickly as possible to the town in grain

and money, Kallias himself sailed at his own expense

to Cyprus and, meeting the king there, with love of honour

on behalf of the city he brought back to the People fifty talents of silver

and twenty thousand medimnoi of wheat as a gift,

which were measured out at Delos to those sent by the

People; and when the king first held the Ptolemaia,

the sacrifice and the competitions for his father, [and the People]

voted to send a sacred delegation and asked [Kallias] to agree

to be chief sacred delegate and to lead [the delegation] on behalf of the People,

Kallias agreed to this with love of honour, and [having waived the]

[fifty] minas voted to him by the People for the chief sacred delegateship

and donated them to the People, he himself [led the delegation]

from his own resources well and [worthily] of the People, [and]

took care of the sacrifice on behalf of the city and of all the other things

which were appropriate together with the sacred delegates; and when the People was then

about to hold the Panathenaia for Archegetis [for the first time] [since]

the town had been recovered, [Kallias], having discussed with the king

the tackle which had to be prepared for the robe, [and]

the king [having donated] it to the city, he made every effort [that]

it should be as fine as possible for the goddess, and that the sacred delegates [elected]

with [him] should immediately bring [the tackle] here; [and]

now, appointed at Halikarnassos by [king]

[Ptolemy], Kallias continues to show love of honour [towards both the]

embassies and the sacred delegations sent by the People [to]

[king] Ptolemy, and [individually taking every]

care [of each of the] citizens who come to him

and of the soldiers [stationed there]

with him, considering most important [the interest]

and collectively the good repute of the city; and [...]

to the fatherland Kallias never endured [...]

when the People had been overthrown, but even gave up [his own]

property to be given as a contribution in the oligarchy, so as to do [nothing]

contrary either to the laws or to the democracy

of all the Athenians; so, therefore, that all [who]

wish to show love of honour towards the city may know that the People [always]

remembers those who have benefited it and renders thanks to each;

for good fortune, the Council shall decide: that the

presiding committee allotted to preside at the Assembly

prescribed by law shall put these matters on the agenda and submit the opinion

of the Council to the People, that it seems good to the Council to

praise Kallias son of Thymochares of Sphettos for his excellence and

good will which he continues to have towards the Athenian People, and

to crown him with a gold crown according to the law, and

to announce the crown at the new tragedy competition

of the Great Dionysia; and the board of administrators shall manage the making of the crown and the

announcement; and the People shall also stand

a bronze statue of him in the agora, and

he shall have a front seat at all the competitions which the city

puts on, and the director of works elected for the sanctuaries

shall assign the front seat to him; and the People shall

elect at once three men from all the Athenians who will manage

both the making of the statue and its setting up; and

the thesmothetai shall bring the scrutiny of the award

for him to the Heliaia when the days required by law

have passed; and so that a reminder may remain also for the future

of Kallias' acts of love of honour towards the People,

the prytany secretary shall inscribe this decree on a

stone stele and stand it beside the statue; and for the

inscribing and the stele the board of administrators shall allocate the

expense accrued.
