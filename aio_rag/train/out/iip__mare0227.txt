King Seleukos to Heliodoros his brother, greetings.

Taking the greatest forethought for the safety of those subject

to us, and considering it the greatest good

for our affairs when those throughout

the kingdom manage their own lives without fear,

and perceiving that nothing can

partake of the prosperity that is fitting without

the goodwill of the gods, (we), so that the sanctuaries established

throughout the other satrapies may receive their ancestral

honours with the appropriate [care],

happen to have made arrangements from the beginning; and since the affairs [in]

[Koi]le Syria and Phoenicia did not have

[anyone] appointed to the care of them, we

[consider]ed that the good order

of the arrangements would be prudently overseen by Olympiodoros, [who], having given us assurance of the regard (we have) for

him from times past,

for, having been brought up with us and having displayed the best

disposition in all things, fittingly

he was appointed over the bedchamber, having shown himself worthy of so great

a trust, and justly he was designated one of the first

friends, having given of his affection towards us

the most earnest proofs; and having been in

such a [position], and having understood how we are

disposed to [increase] the honours of the gods in accordance with

the [policy] of our ancestors [and] the care [of the sanctuaries], [by which]
