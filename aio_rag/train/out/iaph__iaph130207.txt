[Decision of the Council and the]

People, proposal of the gene[rals? and of the]

secretary Hephaistion son of

Hermogenes ⟦secretary⟧: since

Pankrates son of Adrastos, whose ancestors

were good and noble men and had made

promises (of benefactions) and had made dedications

to the People, and he himself, living

in virtue and nobility, con-

tinued his life in the finest con-

duct and good order, and having been elected

also general of the city he con-

tinued (to serve) justly; the

Council and the People decided to praise

Pankrates and to honour him, and

to set up also a painted image of him on a gilded

shield. The (People) of the Tabenoi (did) the same.



The man who has rounded with dignity the turning-post

of life, of greatest name, Pankrati-

des, this tomb holds, traveller;

who left two sons

and a fair daughter, and having seen

in turn her children and their offspring;

honoured among the citizens with offices and council-seats,

having completed seven decades of years and two

years, he brought to an end, unmourned

and without grief, a noble life.

Farewell, having left behind for mortals an

undying memory.
