In the archonship of Heliodoros (229/8), in the eleventh prytany, of Kekropis.

Thargelion. Lysistratos son of Phylarchides

of Oinoe proposed: since Eumaridas, both previously,

at the time when it happened that Boukris, having raided the

country, carried off to Crete many of the citizens and

of the others from the city, provided many great services

to the People, and contributed in advance money

from his own resources towards the twenty talents agreed for

the prisoners, and also lent to those captured for travelling expenses,

and now, when the People despatched ambassadors so that

the friendly relations with all the Cretans should continue and that,

if anywhere a right of seizure had been granted to those sailing in, this

should be removed, he spoke in support so that everything in the interests of

the People should be accomplished, and also joined in the embassy to Knossos and the

allies, and also gave the ambassadors letters to his friends in Polyrrhenia,

so that they would co-operate with

them concerning the People's interests, and he undertakes to take every

care that the friendly relations of the People should continue

towards all those who live in Crete; so, therefore, that the People

may be seen to honour those who display the preference which they have

at every opportunity, for good fortune, the People shall decide:

to praise Eumaridas son of Pankles of Kydonia and to crown

him with a gold crown according to the law for his good will

and love of honour towards the Council and the People

of the Athenians, and also to stand a bronze statue of him on the acropolis;

and if in the future too he provides services, it shall be possible for him

to be honoured worthily of whatever benefactions he may confer; and the secretary

of the People shall inscribe this decree on a stone stele

and stand it beside the statue; and the treasurer of the military fund and the board

of administrators shall allocate the expenditure accrued

for the setting up of the statue and the stele.

In the archonship of Archelaos (228/7). Anthesterion, the intercalary one. Dionysios son of Theophilos

of Elaious proposed: since, because of the good will which he continually

shows towards the People, Eumaridas of Kydonia, previously

the People voted to set up a statue of him, and now Eurykleides

and Mikion, having made known the services which he

provides, request that he be granted its erection in the precinct of the

People and the Graces, for good fortune, the People shall decide:

to grant Eumaridas the erection of the statue, which previously

the People voted, in the precinct of the People and the Graces,

just as Eurykleides and Mikion request for him; and

the other honours voted for him shall also be valid.

In olive crown:

The Council,

the People

(honour) Eumaridas

son of Pankles

of Kydonia.

In the archonship of Phanarchides (193/2), in the third prytany, of Pandionis;

Boedromion. Nikias son of Polyxenos of Poros proposed:

since Charmion, being the son of Eumaridas of Kydonia, who is

proxenos and benefactor, making himself worthy of the

honours given to his father, having sailed into the

Piraeus as a sacred delegate despatched to Delphi by the citizens,

when the generals introduced him, has given an account to the

Council of both his ancestral good will and the things accomplished by himself,

and announces that in the future he will be useful

both individually and collectively to Athenians who come to Crete;

so, therefore, that at every opportunity friendly treatment of those who are worthy

may be performed, now as also previously, for good fortune, the Council

shall decide: that the presiding committee allotted to preside at the forthcoming

Assembly shall put these matters on the agenda, and submit the opinion

of the Council to the People, that it seems good to the Council that the

generals shall send hospitality gifts to Charmion son of Eumaridas and to the

fellow sacred delegates who have come with him and to his son Eumaridas,

and the treasurer of the military fund shall allocate the expenditure

accrued in accordance with the regulation; and he and his descendants shall also have the ancestral

proxeny; and it shall be possible for him, if in the future too he

displays his own preference, to obtain whatever benefit

from the Council and the People he may be deemed worthy of.
