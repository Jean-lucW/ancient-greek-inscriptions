**Part 1**

[Pai]an and p[rosod]ion for t[he God, which] Limeni[os son of Th]oino[s of Athens] [composed and accompanied on the cithara].

**Column 1**

Come to this far-seen Par[nassian] [dance-loving]

twin-peaked slope, and be[gin] [my] hymns,

Pierian Muses, who dwell on the snow-beaten rocks of [Heli]kon;

and sing of the Pythian, golden-haired, [far]-shooter, fine-lyred

Phoibos, whom blessed Leto bore be[side the] famous [lake],

grasping with her hands [a branch] of the grey-green olive [in her birth-pangs],

a luxuriant one.

And all the heavenly vault rejoiced, [cloudless, bright];

and the air held still the swift-flying

courses [of the storm-winds]; and the deep-roaring [furious] swell of Nereus ceased, and great Ocean, who embraces the earth all around with his [moist] arms.

Then, leaving the Kynthian island, the [God] came to first-fruit-bearing famous Attica, on the [crested headland] of Tritonis;

and the Libyan [flute], pouring out a honey-breathing voice, [sang] a sweet tune, mingling with the varied [melodies of the cithara];

and at once the rock-dwelling Ech[o] cried out ['Paian, ie Paian']; and he rejoiced, because, having received in his mind the immortal [thought] of Zeus, [he recognised it]; in return for which,

from that beginning, we call him Paieon, [the whole] autochthonous people and the great thyrsos-striking holy [swarm] of the artists of Bacchus, dwelling in the city of Kekrops. But, you

who hold the oracle-singing tripod, come to [this] God-trodden Parnassian ridge, beloved of the inspired. [And you], around your hair

having woven a branch of [wine-dark] laurel, [and] [boundless foundations]

dragging with immortal hand, lord, [you encounter] the [monstrous]

daughter of Earth. But, lovely-eyed [offspring] of Leto, you slew with arrows the [savage] child of Earth, [and likewise Tityos, because]

**Column 2**

he conceived a desire for your mother [...]

the beast which you killed [...]

the hissing from its lairs [...]

[when] you were guarding [the sacred seat] of Earth, [lord, by the navel], and when the barbarian Ares, [not revering] your oracular [seat rich in treasure], plundering it, perished in the wet [storm of snow]. [But, O Phoibos],

save the God-founded [city] of Pallas [and its famous people, and with them]

the goddess, mistress of the Cretan bows [and hounds, Artemis, and Leto]

most glorious; and [care for] the inhabitants of Delphi [together with their children and] wives, their houses free from harm, and come [graciously] to the servants of Bacchus [the sacred victors], and the spear-[crowned]

rule of the Romans increase [in might], flourishing with ageless [victory].
