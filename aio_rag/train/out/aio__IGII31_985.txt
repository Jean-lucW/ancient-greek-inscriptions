in command of the ships of the city, the citizens

sailing in the ships he got excused, so that of

the allies the Athenians alone were exempt

from the works for the siege; and

Phaidros himself, having the same disposition as his ancestors,

has continued to make himself worthy

of their good will towards the People; and in the archonship of Nikias (296/5)

having been elected general by the People in charge of

the equipment twice, he managed everything that was appropriate

well and with love of honour; and having been elected for the countryside

several times and having been in charge of the mercenaries three times,

he made every effort that the soldiers,

being as well equipped as possible, should render their services

to the People; and having served as ambassador to king

Ptolemy the elder he delivered to the People grain

and money; and having been elected by the People general in charge of the

hoplites for the year of the archonship of Kimon (288/7)

he continued to struggle for the common safety,

and when difficult times surrounded the city he preserved

peace for the countryside, always declaring the best course,

and for the grain and the other crops from the countryside

being brought in he was responsible, having advised the People

and having been elected competition director by the People in the archonship of Nikias

(282/1?) he managed the sacrifices so that they might all be [completed]

according to tradition and that the competitions might [be] as fine as possible

and worthy of the love of honour of the People; and later,

when his son Thymochares had been elected competition director

[for] the year of the archonship of Euboulos, he shared in managing

all these things too, making clear

in everything the good will that he has for the People; and all the other

liturgies he has performed with love of honour,

and in all the public contributions that have taken place among the People

he has participated; and for all these things he has been crowned

by both the Council and the People; so that

therefore the People too may be seen to honour good

men worthy of remembrance, for good fortune,

the Council shall decide that the presiding committee allotted

to preside in the People, when the days

set according to the law have passed, shall put the matter

on the agenda and submit the opinion of the Council to the

People, that it seems good to the Council to praise Phaidros

son of Thymochares of Sphettos and crown him

with a gold crown according to the law for his excellence

and the good will which he continues to have for the

Athenian People; and to announce the crown at the

new tragedy competition of the Great Dionysia

and at the athletic competition of the Great Panathenaia;

and the making of the crown

and the announcement shall be managed by the

administrator; and the People shall also stand

a bronze statue of him in the agora; and he shall have dining rights

in the city hall, as shall the eldest of his descendants

at the time, and a front row seat at all the competitions

that the city puts on; and the People shall

elect immediately three men from the Athenians who

will manage both the making of the statue

and its erection; and this decree

the prytany secretary shall inscribe on a

stone stele and stand it beside the statue;

and for inscribing the stele the administrator

shall allocate the expenditure accrued.

Lyandros son of Lysiades of Anaphlystos proposed: for

good fortune, the People shall decide: to do all the other

things concerning the award which

Phaidros has requested according to the earlier decree which Lyandros

proposed; and the court presidents shall submit for him

the scrutiny of the award to the court

according to the law. For the erection of the

statue these have been elected: Thymochares

of Sphettos, Menon of Acharnai, Straton

of Sphettos.
