In the archonship of Euthios (283/2), in the [third] prytany, of A[kamantis],

for which Nausimenes son of Nausikydes of Cholargos

was secretary. On the eighteenth of Boedromion,

the nineteenth of the prytany. Principal Assembly.

Of the presiding committee Hieromnemon son of Teisimachos

of Koile was putting to the vote and his fellow presiding committee members. The Council and

People decided. Nikeratos son of Phileas of Kephale proposed: since

Philippides has continued at every opportunity

to demonstrate his good will towards the People, and

having gone abroad to king Lysimachos and previously

having discoursed with the king, he brought to the People as a

gift ten thousand Attic medimnoi of wheat, which were

distributed to all Athenians in the archonship of Euktemon (299/8),

and he also discoursed about a yard-arm and mast, so that

they might be given to the goddess for the Panathenaia for the peplos, which were

brought in the archonship of Euktemon; and when king

Lysimachos was victorious in the battle that took place at Ipsos

against Antigonos [and] Demetrios, those

of the citizens who died in the [danger] he buried at

his own expense, and [those] who were taken prisoner,

having made representation to the [king and] obtained their release,

those who wished [to serve in the army] he arranged

to be placed [in] commands, and those who

chose to depart he clothed and gave travelling expenses

from his own resources and sent them where each wished,

being more than three hundred; and he also requested that

those of the citizens be released who had been caught in

Asia, imprisoned by Demetrios and Antigonos;

and he continues to be useful to those Athenians who meet him at any time,

in whatever way each asks him;

and since the People recovered its freedom he has continued

saying and doing what is in the interests of the

preservation of the city, and urging the king to help

with both money and grain, so that the People may remain

free and recover the Piraeus and the

forts as quickly as possible; and concerning all these things

the king has often borne witness to him before the

Athenians serving as ambassadors to him; and having been

[elected] competition director in the archonship of Isaios (284/3), he complied

[with the] People's wishes, volunteering from his own resources, and sacrificed the ancestral

[sacrifices] to the gods on behalf of the People, and

gave [the -] to all Athenians in all the

[competitions, and] established an additional competition for Demeter

[and Kore], the first to do so, as a memorial of the People's

[freedom, and] he also [managed] the other competitions and

[sacrifices on behalf of the city], and having spent on all these from his

[own resources much] money, he has submitted his accounts

according to the laws, and has never [done] anything contrary to

democracy, either [in word or]

deed; in order therefore that it may be clear [to all that the People]

knows how to render thanks [to its benefactors worthy]

of the benefactions they confer, for good [fortune, the]

Council [shall decide], that the presiding committee who are allotted [to preside]

in the People, when the days [specified in the law]

for the request have elapsed, shall put [these matters] on the agenda [at the]

first Assembly according to the law, and [submit] the opinion

of the Council to the People, that it seems good to the Council

to praise Philippides son of Philokles of Kephale

for his excellence and the good will which he continues to have

for the Athenian People, and to crown him with a gold

crown according to the law, and to proclaim the

crown at the tragedy competition of the Great Dionysia;

and to stand a bronze statue of him in the theatre;

and he shall have meals in the city hall, and

whoever is at any time his eldest descendant, and a seat of honour at all

the competitions which the city puts on; and the making

of the crown and the proclamation shall be managed by

the board of administrators; and this decree shall be inscribed

by the prytany secretary on a stone

stele and stood beside the temple of Dionysos; and

for inscribing the stele the board of administrators shall allocate

20 drachmas [from] the People's fund for expenditure

on decrees.

In crown

The People.
