1. [The appeals which] Ael(ius) Praxagoras, Cl(audius) Demostratos, Ael(ius) Themison made against Ael(ius) Dionysios [appear] to be justified; [therefore]

[of these] appeals there was need to hear; it seemed sufficient [to know?] that the elections had been conducted lawfully; [but]

[the judgement of these] men I did not consider it necessary to undertake, since it did not even [take place] at Athens. [For]

the following time, however, so that nothing ambiguous may be left, those who voluntarily [seek] the office of torch-bearer or some other

priesthood greater than the one which they already hold must first lay down their headband lawfully; but if anyone [by]

the People should be called, there will be no dispute against him unless he lays down his previous insignia before he is

appointed. Once elected, however, this man too shall lay down that which he held previously. 2. The appeals which Sentius

Attalos and Clemens son of Clemens and Cl(audius) Chrysippos made from the court and from the king Cl(audius) Eupraxides

against Valerius Mamertinus shall be struck out. Mamertinus, then, since, being a Eumolpid, neither of his

parents was from the genos of the Kerykes, falls short of the only way in which it is permitted for those from either of these two

genē to transfer to the other; he shall refrain from aspiring to the office of sacred herald. And the elections,

among the others, both those who have already had their claims adjudged and those who now wish to stand, according to the laws of the

Athenians, shall be held again. Mamertinus shall not be removed from the number of the Eumolpidai and shall recover his [priesthood].

What occurred to me in this matter, having taken my judgement from the present case, would not [for]

the future confound the established safeguards. 3. Ladikos son of Polyainos, who appealed against Sophanes son of Sophanes

from Iul(ius) Damostratos the archon of the Panhellenes, appears, within the appointed time [already]

within which it is possible to bring a case against those elected Panhellenes, to have been summoned to judgement, but having been refuted,

although the election took place after the customary deadline, as not yet having reached the legal

age even then and not having previously held any office, as the God my grandfather determined, [to have appealed] unjustly,

it seems. 4. Epigonos son of Epiktetos, having appealed from Iul(ius) Damostratos against Eudemos son of Aphrodisios,

shall contest before my Quintilii concerning membership of the Panhellenion; for he himself asked to be sent to the judgement [before]

them, and it is clear that many of the parts which fall to me in the affairs of the [province]

are carried out by them. 5. Athenodoros son of Agrippa, having appealed from Papius Rufus, who was archon of the Panhellenes

in the previous four-year period, against the administrators of the estate of the most excellent Cl(audius) Herodes, since he produced neither [the]

records of the Panhellenes nor the verdict that was delivered, shall contest before my Quintilii,

so that he may not be compelled to wait so long for the occasions on which it will be possible for me, alongside military operations, to adjudicate matters requiring judgement.

6. Nostimos son of Dionysios did not show that he had become an [Areopagite] according to the laws,

but whether he is in such a position as to be able to be enrolled in the Areopagos Council, the Quintilii will [decide];

for that he should be expelled from membership of the council of the Panhellenes [seems to have been]

shown to me when judging the appeal made against Euphras son of Nikon. 7. Popilius [Peios]

shall remain in the right of Athenian citizenship, as the Areopagites decided; for [of his status]

he must have security, and so must the others who, following the authority granted owing to the destruction [of a]

copy, presented their own rights to the Areopagites; and for the future, [in accordance with]

the laws and the ancestral customs, it shall be guarded and scrutinised whether anyone is [an Athenian] by descent.

8. The appeal which Ael(ius) Praxagoras made from Gavinius Saturninus, in the case [in which],

the so-called codicils in the name of Stratolaos having been produced, the inquiry took place, appears to be justified.

If, however, anyone wishes, within two months after the reading out of

this letter at Athens, either publicly or privately, to bring a case concerning the gift which they claimed to be owed to the city,

let it be permitted to him, a further period of two months being preserved for the decision

before my Quintilii; but if he should fall short in this intervening period, he should not attempt at a later time

to resume the contest. Praxagoras shall return to the possession of the estates [and]

shall receive from the most excellent Quintilii an arbitrator concerning the produce; and to the estates which were said to have been left to the most excellent Herodes

he shall return at once; concerning the produce Ingenuus shall judge; and if Praxagoras complains of slowness

of handover, (the case) shall be introduced by the governors of the province. To the request of the Athenians,

which they made in view both of the age of the father and of his being the only one left to the household, I could

yield, so that Pheidimos shall be recalled from the island to which he had been exiled in accordance with the investigation of the matter,

but shall keep away from the borders of Attica in future. 9. To Agathokles son of Agathokles (is granted) the request through the embassy [which]

he is pursuing with me; he shall be restored to his fatherland and to the council of the Areopagites; and the sureties for the appealed

cases which the administrators of the estate of the most excellent Herodes made against Ael(ius) Ameinias, and likewise the sureties for appeals [which]

were made in the cases spoken before me by Ael(ius) Praxagoras, Cl(audius) Demostratos, Ael(ius) Themison, Sentius

Attalos, Clemens son of Clemens, Valerius Mamertinus, Ladikos son of Polyainos, Euodos son of Onesimos, Nostimos son of

Dionysios, Popilius Peios, shall be returned. The appeals of Epigonos and Athenodoros, that they must be struck out,

I have already said previously. If any other appeals dependent on this decision, concerning which I made nothing clear,

had been made before the verdict, they shall be struck out, so that they are examined before the judge in the same way

as they were also going to be examined if there were no appeal case; and whose they are Ingenuus shall write

to me; and if any were lodged after the verdict, he himself shall hear these in the appropriate

order. 10. How much zeal I contribute for the reputation of Athens, so that it may retain its ancient

dignity, I think I have shown sufficiently, also when I attempted to restore their select council

to the ancient custom, according to which they admitted to the Areopagos only those

who had been examined for three generations of descent; and would that there were an abundance of the illustrious families, so that it might be possible for me [even now]

still to maintain our judgement; but since, taking account of what has happened through fortune,

in which cases I know that many other cities too have claimed care to the greatest degree, it is necessary to relax some things

out of humanity towards the past, and to establish others adequately for the future, agreeing to this extent

I shall concede to the Athenians that for the past time attention is paid only to this, whether [anyone] has

a well-born father, and if any of the Areopagites appears to have been expelled because of the rule of three generations, he shall recover the rank,

but after this it will suffice for someone to be of well-born parents on both sides. For those who after our letter,

being of a freedman father, have thrust themselves into the Areopagos, it is time to cease from ignorance;

if, however, anyone of a freedman father before the letter was expelled by no court

of those able to be assigned to these matters, he shall remain. Those who after our letter, because their grandfather

had been freed, either were removed from the Areopagos Council or voluntarily withdrew from the honour, are still

excluded; but those who will withdraw from the council because of the aforesaid must in no way because of this

be aggrieved with regard to seeking the other honours and ranks. If any have been enrolled contrary to what we have

instructed, for whom even after the verdict now delivered it is not possible to sit in the Areopagos,

let them depart within a year; and whoever even after the prohibition continues to remain [or]

intrudes contrary to what has been written above, shall be ranked equally with those removed through the court, [and shall be]

inferior because of this also in seeking the lesser ranks and honours. If any of the Areopagites who are among the [Panhellenes]

today are found to be unable to provide (proof of) three generations, they shall not on this account be expelled

from the council; but for the future no other of the Areopagites shall be enrolled among the Panhellenes

than those who are able to come to the elections having the three generations. As regards the Council

of the Five Hundred, it is sufficient for it to be arranged in this way, that those enrolled themselves be

well-born. 11. Ingenuus himself shall be judge of the disputes from each of the parties, and he shall also judge

claims against those who take care of the estate of the most excellent man, as many as do not themselves make counter-claims.

Those who have administered the public funds of the city and owe accounts have as judges

my Quintilii, before whom the advocates of the Athenians shall see to it that they recover their [amounts].

Ingenuus shall judge those of the Athenians requiring judgement at Eleusis and in Piraeus, at whatever time

he wishes, elsewhere; and for those from Sparta he shall fix a place in the neighbouring cities, as is

most suitable, having judged it himself. 12. I believe it has been made clear, through what I have declared, that I have provided for each thing not out of

abundance rather than of care, so that for the future it may be possible for Herodes to share in good cheer among the Athenians

concerning both divine and human matters, together with his illustrious zeal for education, and that the Athenians,

mindful of the formerly known benefaction of the most excellent Herodes towards them, may renew the confidence of their former

intimacy with one another, because of which they do not even need me as a reconciler. For what could still lurk in anyone's

mind, after the memory of the charges has been erased, the care having been carried out in all respects [so that]

it may be possible for the Athenians to love my Herodes, who is also their own, with no other [great] obstacle any longer

opposing their good will? 13. What, however, in addition to all these things drawn up in the language of the Greeks concerning the judgements,

I further considered as omitted in what I declared, which could be understood from my intention, even if not from

the words of the verdict, but requires some explanation, so that no-one may raise a [new]

dispute, it seemed proper to define this too clearly: if any born of freedman fathers

withdrew from the Council of the Five Hundred, let it be granted to them to return again to the scrutiny

in the same way as also to those of the Areopagos; but let those of the Council of the

Five Hundred prove that they themselves were born in freedom; for not those of freedman fathers

but only those who have themselves been freed should be excluded, which has also been precisely determined for the future concerning

them.
