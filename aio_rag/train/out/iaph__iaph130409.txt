The Council and the People [decided ...] [...] Ma[rsyas]

son of Apollonios son of Marsyas, secretary of the People, and

[...], general in charge of the territory: since Hermogenes son of Hephaistion

Theodotos, (one) of the first and most distinguished citizens, his ancestors

being among the greatest and among those who held the People together, and having lived in

virtue and acts of love of glory and very many promises (of benefactions) and the finest

deeds towards the fatherland, and he himself having

proved a noble and good man and a lover of his fatherland and founder and benefactor

of the city and saviour, and having conducted himself with good will and prudence

both towards the whole People and towards each one of the

citizens, and being most piously disposed towards the gods and towards the

fatherland, having adorned it with the greatest love of glory with the finest

promises and dedications, and having been appointed to many embassies, and those most necessary,

and to contests, he accomplished in the finest manner

everything; and having undertaken all the magistracies and having been elected

many times with distinction, he conducted himself justly and cleanly; and with

the authorities and the governors having the greatest acquaintance and

standing, he benefited the city greatly through these too; and having been chosen

also as stephanephoros he performed this liturgy too

in a manner befitting the sacred and with decorum; for all of which the People, having approved him,

rendered to him the thanks he deserved; and now he has departed

this life, and it is fitting that his funeral procession too should be distinguished;

the Council and the People shall decide to praise him even after his

death and that he be crowned by the Council and the People

with a crown of valour of one hundred gold pieces.

Hermogenes son of Hephaistion Theodotos, farewell.
