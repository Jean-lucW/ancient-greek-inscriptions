In the archonship of Charikles (196/5), in the ninth prytany, of Aigeis, for which

Aischrion son of Euainetos of Rhamnous was secretary. Decrees of the People.

On the thirteenth of Elaphebolion, but according to divine reckoning the eighteenth, the twenty-eighth

of the prytany. Principal Assembly in Piraeus. Of the presiding committee was putting

to the vote Antipatros son of Potamon of Lamptrai and his fellow presiding committee members.

The Council and the People decided.

Sodamos son of Timasitheos of Oion proposed: since Kephisodoros, having displayed zealous

good will towards the People at every opportunity,

and having been active in public life for nearly thirty years honestly and incorruptibly,

and having shirked neither any toil nor any danger for the sake of

the common interest, and having undertaken all the other liturgies

to which the People appointed him, and having also been treasurer

of the military fund well and with love of glory, and having carried out jointly the treasurership

of the grain-purchase fund as one of three in the year of Apollodoros and

Proxenides, and having also enacted beneficial laws

for the concord of all Athenians, and having advised on equal and

just sources of revenue, and having also proposed the means by which

the People will keep its existing friends steadfast, remaining in their loyalty,

and will acquire others in addition, and having foreseen the plots arising

from outsiders and having taken it upon himself to resist them,

and having advised good and advantageous

alliances for the People, and having served on embassies about the most

important matters for the safety of the cities and the countryside, and having brought

money and grain and not a few other gifts, and having also proposed

many decrees that were useful and brought glory and success and

dignity to the People, and having remained in the same

policy all the time, hating wickedness, and through the continuity

of what was done and devised having been most of all responsible,

with the good favour of the gods, for the People's preserving

its autonomy, and for not a few of the other Greeks having

[secured] the greatest of benefits; and on account of all these things [showing]

himself deserving to obtain honour according to the law, he requests

[now that] the People [stand] a bronze statue of him in the agora and

[another in Piraeus] in the trading-post, and dining rights for himself in the city

[hall, and for the eldest of his descendants at] the time, and a front row seat at all

[the competitions that the city] puts on; and he has submitted the request to

[the Council, containing] the details of what he has done;

[in order therefore that the People may be seen] to honour the good men and

[worthy ones, and that others also may become] emulators of such a policy, (knowing) that

[they always] obtain [from the People of the Athenians] the appropriate

[honour, for good fortune,] the Council shall decide: that the presiding

[committee allotted to preside at] the principal Assembly

[shall bring him before the People and put] these matters [on the agenda],

[and submit the opinion of the Council to the People, that it seems good to the]

[Council] to praise [Kephisodoros [...] and]

[crown] him with a gold [crown according to the law for the excellence and good will]

[which he] continues [to have towards the People] of [the Athenians, and to announce]

this [crown at the City Dionysia in the new tragedies]

[and the Pana]thenaia and [the Eleusinia and] the Ptol[emaia at the gymnastic]

[competitions]; and the [generals] and the treasurer [of the military fund]

[shall take care] of the [making of the] crown [and its announcement];

and to stand [also] a bronze statue [of him in the agora and another in]

[Piraeus] in the [trading-post]; and he shall have [dining rights in the city]

[hall], as shall the eldest of his descendants at the time, [and a front row seat at all]

[competitions that the city] puts on; and the court presidents [shall introduce the]

[scrutiny for him, when] the days [set by the] law have passed [...]
