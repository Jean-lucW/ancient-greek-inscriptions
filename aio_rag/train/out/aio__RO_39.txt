**Part 1**

Gods.

In the archonship of Charikleides (363/2). Aiantis was in prytany. Nikostratos of Pallene was secretary. Philittios of Boutadai

was chairman. The Council and the People decided. Aristophon

proposed: since the Ioulietans whom the Athenians restored demonstrate that the city of the Ioulietans owes to the city

of the Athenians three talents from the money reckoned up according to the decree of the People of the Athenians which Menexenos proposed, the People shall decide: the Ioulietans shall repay this money to the Athenians in the month of Skirophorion in the archonship of Charikleides (363/2); and if they do not pay in the time stated, those who have been chosen by the People to exact the money owed from the islanders shall exact it from them in whatever way they know how, and the generals of the Ioulietans, Echetimos and Nikoleos and Satyros and Glaukon and Herakleides, shall join them in exacting it; and so that the oaths and the agreement which Chabrias the general made and swore to the Keians on behalf of the Athenians and of the Keians whom the Athenians restored shall be valid, the generals of the Ioulietans who are named in the

decree to join in exacting the money shall inscribe them on a stone stele and stand it in the sanctuary of Apollo Pythios, just as they are inscribed at Karthaia; and

the secretary of the Council shall also inscribe them on a stele in the same way and

stand it on the acropolis, and for the inscribing the treasurer of the People shall give 20 drachmas from the fund for expenditure on decrees. And since those of the Ioulietans who contravened the oaths and the agreement and made war against the People of the Athenians and the Keians and the other allies, and, after they had been condemned to death, returned to

Keos, both threw down the stelai on which were inscribed the agreement with the Athenians and the names of those who contravened the oaths and the agreement; and of the friends of the Athenians whom the People restored they killed some, and others

they condemned to death and confiscated their property contrary to

the oaths and the agreement (Satyrides and Timoxenos and Miltiades), because they had accused Antipatros, when the Council of the Athenians condemned him to death for having killed the proxenos of the Athenians Aision contrary to the decrees of the People of the Athenians and having contravened the oaths and the agreement: they shall be exiled from Keos and Athens and their property shall be public property of the People of the Ioulietans; and the generals of the Ioulietans who are present in Athens shall register their names immediately with the secretary in the presence of the People; and if [any of those] registered dispute that they are among these [men], it shall be possible for them, after providing guarantors before the generals of the Ioulietans within thirty days, to submit to trial according to the oaths and the

agreement in Keos and [in the] appeal city at Athens; and Satyrides and Timo[xenos and] M[iltia]des shall go back to Keos to their own property; and to praise the Ioulietans who have come, Demetrios, Herakle[ide]s, E[cheti]mos, K[al]liphantos; and to praise also Satyr[ides] and [Tim]o[xeno]s and Miltiades;

and to praise also the city of the Karthaians and Aglokritos; and to invite them to hospitality in the city hall

tomorrow.

The generals of the Athenians and the allies made this agreement and swore it with the cities on Keos: 'I shall not bear a grudge

for anything that has happened in the past against the Keians, nor shall I kill any [of the Keians], nor shall I make an exile of any of those who abide by these oaths [and] agreement, but I shall bring them into the alliance [just like] the other allies; and if anyone attempts any revolution

[in Keos contrary to] the oaths and the agreement, I shall not permit it by any [means] or device whatsoever as far as possible; and if anyone

[does not wish to] live on Keos, I shall allow him to live in whichever of the [allied] cities he wishes and to enjoy the fruits of his own property. These things

[I shall abide by on oath, by] Zeus, by Athena, by Poseidon, by

[Demeter; if I keep the oath] may there be many good things, but if I swear falsely, [bad things.' Oaths and agreement] of the cities on Keos with [the Athenians and the allies] and of the Keians whom the Athenians restored: '[I shall be an ally to the Athenians and] the allies, and I shall not [revolt from the Athenians and] the allies, neither myself

[nor shall I obey anyone else, as far as possible]; and all the private suits and [public suits against Athenians I shall make] subject to appeal [according to the agreement, as many as are over a] hundred drachmas; and if [anyone dares to wrong those of the Keians who have returned] or the Athenians [or any of the allies contrary to the oaths and] the agreement,

[I shall not permit it by any means or device] whatsoever, but I shall help [with all my strength as far as possible. These things I shall] abide by on oath, by [Zeus, by Athena, by Poseidon, by] Demeter; if I [keep the oath may there be many good things, but if I swear falsely,] bad things.'
