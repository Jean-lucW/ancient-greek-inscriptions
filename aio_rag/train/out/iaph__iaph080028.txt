Copy from the privileges granted by the Emperors and the Senate and People of the Romans:

nor indeed shall any magistrate or promagistrate of the Roman People or anyone else, into the city or the

territory or the boundaries of the Plarasans and Aphrodisians, a soldier or a substitute soldier, a cavalryman

or anyone else be assigned to them for winter quarters, nor order them to deposit either money

or soldiers, or ships, or grain, or weapons, or rafts, nor indeed shall any other thing

be imposed on the People of the Plarasans and ⟦Aphrodisians⟧.

Copy of the oath made between the Romans and the Plarasans ⟦Aphrodisian⟧s, with 340 senators present:

a prefect and a garrison within the city of the Plarasans and ⟦Aphrodisi⟧ans and into their own territory, whatever

territory of theirs is their private property, they shall not receive against their will; revenues (and) tributes they shall not pay.

And that at the contests and the gladiatorial combats, and also hunts and if athletes compete in the city of Rome and near the city of Rome

within one mile, it shall be permitted for the ambassadors of the Plarasans and ⟦Aphrodisi⟧ans to sit and watch in the place of the senators; and whatever

ambassadors of the Plarasans and ⟦Aphrodisians⟧ come at any time to Rome to the Senate, they shall report to the magistrates (and) promagistrates

of the Roman People who have the power to convene the Senate, so that the Senate may be granted to them; it is resolved that out of

turn the Senate be granted to them and that they have the right to speak in that body and to report, and that within the ten days

nearest to those on which they approach (and) report, a reply be given to the ambassadors of the Plarasans and Aphrodisians.
