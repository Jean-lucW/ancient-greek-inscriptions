In the (stephanephorate) of Claudia Paulina.

[Emper]or Caesar, son of the God Trajan Parthicus, grandson of the God Nerva, Trajan

[Hadria]n Augustus, pontifex maximus, tribunician power for the third time,

[cons]ul [for] the third time (119), to the magistrates of the Aphrodisians and the Council and the People,

[gree]tings. Your freedom and autonomy and the other (privileges) given

to you both by the Senate and by the Emperors before me, I

confirmed earlier. Having been petitioned through an embassy concerning the use

of iron and the tax on nails, although the

matter is disputable, since it is not now for the first time that the tax-collectors have

attempted to collect from you too, nevertheless, knowing that the city both in other respects is

worthy of honour, and has been removed from the framework of the province,

I release it from the payment and I have written to Claudius

Agrippinus, my procurator, to instruct the contract[or]

for the tax on iron in Asia to keep away from your city.

Farewell.

In the (stephanephorate) of Claudius Hypsikles the hero. Emperor Caesar,

[s]on of the God Trajan Parthicus, grandson of the God Nerva, Trajan Hadrian

Augustus, pontifex maximus, tribunician power for the ninth time,

consul for the third time (125), to the magistrates of the Aphrodisians and the Council and

the People, greetings. The revenues which you assigned to the bringing of the water

I confirm. And since there were some citizens of yours saying

that, though unable, they had been nominated for the high priesthood, I have sent them

back to you, for you to examine whether, though able to perform liturgies, they are

evading them, or are telling the truth. If, however, some of them should appear to be

wealthier, it is just that those men hold the high priesthood first. I grant you, from the

high priests, to take money instead of gladiatorial shows, and I not only grant it

but also praise the decision. Those who will be chosen by you as superintendents

of the aqueduct, concerning whatever matters they need advice and assistance on, will be

able to approach my procurator Pompeius Severus, to whom I too have

written. Farewell.
