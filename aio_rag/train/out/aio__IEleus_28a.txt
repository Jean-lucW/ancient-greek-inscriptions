[Timo]teles of Acharnai was secretary.

The Council and People decided; Kekropis held the prytany, Timote-

[les] was secretary, Kykneas was chairman. The drafters drafted the following:

the Athenians shall offer first-fruits of the harvest to the Two Goddesses according to ancestral custom and

the oracle from Delphi, from each hundred medimnoi of

barley not less than a sixth (of a medimnos), and of wheat from each hundred medimnoi not

less than a twelfth; and if anyone produces more harvest than this, or as much,

or less, he shall offer first-fruits in the same proportion. The demarchs shall collect it

deme by deme and hand it over to the religious officials

from Eleusis at Eleusis. They shall build three storage pits at Eleusis

according to ancestral custom wherever it seems suitable to the religious officials and the architect,

from the money of the Two Goddesses; and the harvest

they shall put in there which they receive from the demarchs;

and the allies too shall offer first-fruits in the same way. The cities shall choose collectors

of the harvest, in whatever way seems to them best for the harvest

to be collected; and when it has been collected, they shall send it to Athens;

and those who have brought it shall hand it over to the religious officials from Eleusis

at Eleusis; and if they do not accept it within five days

after it has been announced, when those from the city from which the

harvest comes hand it over, the religious officials shall be penalised at their accounting a thousand drachmas

each; and they shall accept it from the demarchs in the same way. Heralds

the Council shall choose and send to the cities announcing

what has been decided by the People, for the present as quickly as possible, and for the

future whenever it seems good to it; and the hierophant and the

torch-bearer shall urge the Greeks at the Mysteries to offer first-fruits of the harvest according to

ancestral custom and the oracle from Delphi; and having written up on a

board the measure of the harvest, both that from the demarchs deme

by deme and that from the cities city by city,

they shall set it up in the Eleusinion at Eleusis and in the Council-

chamber; and the Council shall announce also to all the other Greek

cities, wherever it seems possible to it, telling

them how the Athenians and the allies offer first-fruits, not

commanding them, but urging them to offer first-fruits, if they wish,

according to ancestral custom and the oracle from Delphi; and the religious officials shall receive it

also from these cities, if anyone brings it, in the

same way. They shall sacrifice from the sacrificial cake as the Eumolpidai

expound, and a triple offering led by a bovine with gilded horns to each of the Two Goddesses

from the barley and the wheat, and to Triptolemos and to the God

and to the Goddess and to Eubolos a full-grown victim each, and

to Athena an ox with gilded horns; and the rest of the barley and wheat

the religious officials shall sell together with the Council, and set up dedications

to the Two Goddesses, making whatever the People of Athens decides,

and inscribe on the dedications that they were dedicated from the harvest of the first-fruits,

and (the name) of the Greek who offered the first-fruits; and for those who do these things

there shall be many good things, and good and abundant harvests, whoever

do not wrong the Athenians nor the city of the Athenians nor the Two Goddesses.

Lampon proposed: in other respects as the drafted text on the first-fruits of the

harvest for the Two Goddesses; and the drafted text and this decree

the secretary of the Council shall inscribe on two stone stelai

and set one up at Eleusis in the sanctuary and the other on

the acropolis; and the official sellers shall let out the contract for the two stelai; and the payment

officers shall give the money. These things about the first-fruits of the harvest for

the Two Goddesses shall be inscribed on the two stelai; and a month, Hekatombaion, shall be intercalated

by the new archon. And the king shall mark out the sanctuaries in the

Pelargikon, and in future altars shall not be established in the

Pelargikon without the Council and People, nor shall stones be cut from the

Pelargikon, nor earth nor stones be taken out of it; and if anyone transgresses

any of these things, he shall pay five hundred drachmas, and the king shall

denounce him to the Council; and concerning the first-fruits of olive oil, Lampon shall draft

a text and show it to the Council in the ninth prytany;

and the Council shall be obliged to bring it before the People.
