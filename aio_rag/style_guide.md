# AIO Translation Style Guide

## Personal names
- Transliterate Greek names directly, keeping k and -os/-es: "Euxenos son of Kallias", "Deinostratos son of Deinides", "Diokles", "Theokles". Do not Latinise (not Euxenus, Callias).
- Give genitive patronymics in the nominative form: Καλλίου becomes "son of Kallias", Ἰσχυρίου becomes "son of Ischyrias".
- Latinise only very familiar names and places: "Piraeus", "Boeotia", "Athens", "Philip", "Chios"; Lakedaimonioi becomes "the Spartans".
- Roman names stay in Latin form: "Gaius Poppaeus Attalos son of Gaius Poppaeus Zenon", "Tiberius Claudius", "Vibullus [Polydeukion]". Expand abbreviated praenomina in parentheses: "T(itus) Aelius", "L(ucius)".
- Gods keep Greek forms: "Hermes", "Asklepios", "Hygieia", "Athena Polias", "Dionysos Lenaios".
- Demotics: "of" plus the deme name, never an adjective: "of Aixone", "of Lamptrai", "of Kephisia". Ἐξ Οἴου may be "from Oion" or "of Oion"; Ἀλωπεκῆθεν becomes "of Alopeke".
- Ethnics: "of Aphrodisias", "Herodes of Athens", "Ketriporis the Thracian", "Grabos the Illyrian".
- Collective deme and city names become plural nouns: "the Ikarians", "the Rhamnousians", "the Piraeans", "the Prasieis", "the Chians". In lists the deme heading is the place name ("Kydathenaion", "Prasiai").
- Women: "Tertia daughter of Lucius", "Aristion daughter of Neophilos of Marathon", "wife of Popillius Pius". No comma before "son of" or "daughter of".
- νεώτερος becomes "the younger": "Philios son of Philios the younger".

## Dating formulae
- Prescript elements are separate verbless sentences: "Archon Ourias. In the second prytany, of Aiantis, for which Euxenos son of Kallias of Aixone was secretary. On the penultimate day of Metageitnion, the twenty-eighth of the prytany. Assembly."
- ἐπὶ X ἄρχοντος becomes "In the archonship of X". Also "in the year that Apolexis ... was archon" and "In the priesthood of Phanostratos".
- Chairman: "Of the presiding committee Diokles son of Ischyrias of Ptelea was putting to the vote and his fellow presiding committee members."
- Transliterate months (Metageitnion, Skirophorion, Hekatombaion, Mounichion). Write day numbers out in words: "on the nineteenth of Hekatombaion", "On the eleventh of the prytany".
- After archon names, add the Julian year in parentheses, abbreviated with a slash and no "BC": "Lysimachides (339/8)", "Iason who was after Polykleitos (109/8)". Mark doubt with "?": "(17/16?)". "(324/3 BC)" appears only rarely.

## Decree formulae (set renderings)
- Enactment: "The Council and People decided." / "The Council and the People decided."
- Motion: "Leon son of Kichesias of Aixone proposed:", with a colon. A missing name becomes "name proposed:".
- Resolution: ἐψηφίσθαι becomes "shall resolve" ("the Ikarians shall resolve", "the People shall resolve:"); δεδόχθαι becomes "shall decide" ("the Council shall decide that", "the Rhamnousians shall decide:").
- ἀγαθῇ τύχῃ becomes "for good fortune" (or "for the good fortune of the People of Athens").
- Motivation: ἐπειδή becomes "since"; περὶ ὧν becomes "concerning what ... say".
- Probouleuma: "the presiding committee allotted to preside at the next Assembly shall present them and put the matter on the agenda and submit the opinion of the Council to the People, that it seems good to the Council to ..."
- Infinitives of command become "to praise", "to crown", "to invite". Third-person imperatives and subject-plus-infinitive clauses become "shall": "the demarch ... shall take care of the sacrifice".
- Praise: "to praise ... for their love of honour for the Council and People"; "for his excellence and justice towards the demesmen"; "for his excellence and good will"; "because they are good men with regard to the People of Athens".
- Crowns: "crown him with a foliage crown" (θαλλοῦ στεφάνῳ).
- Hospitality: "invite him to hospitality in the city hall tomorrow"; for citizens, "invite him to dinner in the city hall tomorrow".
- Inscribing: "the prytany secretary shall inscribe this decree on a stone stele and stand it [in the agora]".
- Payment: "for inscribing the stele the board of administrators shall allocate 10 drachmas"; "the treasurer of the military fund shall allocate the expense accrued"; "the administrator [shall allocate the accrued expenditure]".
- Other: ἐξεῖναι becomes "it shall be possible for"; ὁ βουλόμενος becomes "anyone who wishes"; "if this decree lacks anything, the Council shall be authorised"; "These were chosen as ambassadors:".

## Offices, institutions, festivals
- Translate: Council, People, Assembly (capitalised); "presiding committee", "prytany secretary", "general", "cavalry commander" (hipparch), "commander of a tribal cavalry contingent" (phylarch), "commanders of tribal regiments" (taxiarchoi, though "taxiarchs" also occurs), "religious officials" (hieropoioi), "superintendents" (epimeletai), "board of administrators", "treasurer", "captain" (lochagos), "instructors", "keyholder and fire-bearer", "basket-bearer", "public slave", "friendly society", "city hall" (prytaneion), "headquarters" (strategion), "Areopagos Council", "Council of the Five Hundred", "proconsul", "the King" (Persian).
- Transliterate: demarch, demesmen, prytany, ephebes, trierarch, arrhephoros, stele/stelai, agora, acropolis (lower case), Areopagites, tribe names (Aiantis, Oineis, Hippothontis), and festivals (Basileia, Epitaphia, Nemesia, Eleusinia, Epidauria, trieteris).
- Translate descriptive terms: "torch-race", "athletic competition", "precinct", "stage-building", "seat of honour", "property-based tax" (eisphora).
- Roman imperial titles use Latin forms: "Augustus", "Pius Felix", "pontifex maximus", "tribunician power", "acclaimed emperor for the 8th time", "father of the fatherland", "the God Hadrian", "the Fiscus".

## Money, measures, numerals
- Acrophonic numerals become Arabic figures with "dr." and "ob.": "to Neokles: 162 dr.", "1093 dr. 6 ob.", "mortgaged for 700 dr.", "weighing 150 dr.".
- If a numeral may be incomplete, prefix "(≥)": "(≥) 161 dr.".
- Numbers written as words in the Greek stay as words in running text: "three hundred drachmas extra", "eight drachmas two obols", "fifty amphorae", "five men". Decree sums may use figures ("10 drachmas", "1000 talents").
- Keep the ancient units (drachmas, obols, talents, amphorae). Do not convert them.

## Editorial conventions
- Square brackets enclose restored text, mirroring the Greek: "[in the agora]", "[of the Ikarians?]".
- "[...]" marks any lost stretch, whatever its length. Do not count letters or dots.
- A hyphen or en dash marks the lost part of a name or word: "-iades", "Hege-", "-nes son of Diophon", "Mnesarch- of -". A lone "-" stands for a lost word: "the - of the demesmen". "[.]" marks one lost letter: "Meg[.]on".
- Uncertain restorations take "?" inside the bracket ("[hand over the ships?]"). Uncertain readings or renderings take "(?)" or a bare "?": "Chapter of the law-code (?)", "torch? [...]".
- Dotted (uncertain) letters are not marked in English. Illegible letters become "traces".
- vacat becomes "uninscribed space", "1 line uninscribed" or "Uninscribed lines". lacuna stays "lacuna"; also "Uncertain number of lines missing", "Ca. 10 lines missing".
- Keep erasures in ⟦ ⟧: "Priest ⟦Ameipsias son of Archestratos⟧". Silently omit dittographies and deleted letters ({ }, 〚 〛).
- Physical headings in bold: "**Part 1**", "**Fragment a**", "**Face A**", "**Column 1**". Plain labels: "Relief", "In crown", "On moulding", "(a) Upper moulding", "Inscription I".
- Parentheses mark words added for sense, especially implied verbs on dedications: "(dedicated this to) Hermes", "(This statue of)", "(was erected by)", "The Council, the People (crown him)", "(honoured her)". Also used for glosses: "(scil. money)", "(i.e the oil)", "(the)".

## Layout, register, word order
- Translate line by line. Each English line matches one Greek line as closely as English allows, with a blank line between lines. Keep Greek word order where possible, even when it splits phrases: "Euxenos son of Kallias of Aixone / was secretary."
- Be literal but readable. Keep participles ("having been victorious", "having been general", "having won the torch-race at the Epitaphia") and the Greek clause structure.
- A decree is one long sentence. Separate clauses with semicolons and start each new provision with "and": "; and the prytany secretary shall inscribe ...".
- Put oaths and direct speech in single quotes: "'I swear by Zeus and Earth and Sun ...".
- Use British spelling ("honour", "authorised", "despatched"). Capitalise Council, People, Assembly, the King and Gods (as a heading); write lower-case "king Antigonos".
- Do not add commentary or explanatory expansions beyond the minimal parenthetical supplements above.
